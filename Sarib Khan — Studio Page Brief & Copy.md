# Sarib Khan — Studio Page Brief & Copy

Oct 12, 2026 · @Sarib Khan

## 1. Context

This is a single, quiet, beautifully made page that replaces every portfolio link, pitch deck and "here's my work" message Sarib sends. It is not a lead-gen page and it will never run ads. It exists to be opened from a DM or an email by one founder at a time, explored slowly, and remembered.

**Who sends it.** Sarib Khan, founder of Xtarc, a designer based in Karachi who works across UI/UX, motion, branding, web and product, and who also understands funnels, copy and sales. He builds in Framer and Figma and ships custom coded tools with Claude Code (Next.js, Supabase, Tailwind, shadcn/ui).

**Who receives it.** Founders of coaching and info-product businesses that sell high-ticket programs ($3k to $10k) through an application and call funnel, usually with a Skool community, a team of setters and closers, and often a faith-forward or halal positioning. IMA Accelerator is the reference client. Remote Rizq (halal remote appointment setting, "$1K to $5K a month in 45 days", built on a Lovable template) is the reference prospect: a strong offer and real results sitting inside a page that does not look like it.

**The one job of the page.** Make that founder feel two things in order. First, a little flattered and slightly out of their league, as in "why is someone at this level reaching out to me?" Second, after walking through it, the quiet realisation that their $5k offer is being sold in clothes that cost $50, and that saying no to fixing it would be the dumb move.

**What it is not.**

- Not a generic agency site with hero, features, about us and a contact form.
- Not a hard-sell funnel. No countdowns, no "book now" every scroll, no fake scarcity.
- Not personalised by name. It feels written for them because it speaks their world (applications, setters, Skool, pre-call pages, wins channels), so the same link works for every prospect in the niche.

**How it gets used.** Sarib sends a short personal message with the link, for example: "Saw your program, the results are legit. I design for coaching brands like IMA Accelerator. Put this together so you can see how I think, no pitch attached." The page does the rest. It also becomes the link in his bio, his email signature and the thing he screen-shares on calls.

**What it replaces.** The current page at [xtarc.agency/sarib-khan](https://xtarc.agency/sarib-khan) ("Funnel Designer", 37+ clients, six Framer funnel thumbnails, a tools strip and a WhatsApp button). Everything on it carries over, but it is too thin and too template-shaped to do this job.

## 2. What we take from Wireframe

[Wireframe](https://wireframe.co/) works because it behaves like a calm product, not a marketing site. James McDonald's studio page is a narrow column of confident writing inside a quiet interface shell, and the work and names do the selling. We copy the posture, not the pixels.

| Wordmark with a type specimen "Aa" next to it | A designer signature in the logo itself | "Sarib Khan" set with a small interactive specimen that cycles the faces used on the page |

| One large testimonial card with a background image and logo | Proof as a single, heavy object | A rotating testimonial card (voice-note or Loom style), one at a time |

The big addition Wireframe does not have: **working, touchable proof**. Their audience buys craft. Ours buys outcomes, so the page has to let them play with the actual kind of things Sarib builds (a funnel map, a calculator, a quiz step, a coach booking card) right inside the interface.

## 3. Positioning and psychology

**Core thesis:** in the coaching world almost nobody competes on design, so whoever does wins by default. A $5k offer can be sold through an ugly funnel, but it sells better, closes easier and is remembered longer when every step a lead touches feels considered.

**Positioning line (internal):** Sarib is the designer behind the funnels, communities and internal tools of serious coaching brands. He does not sell websites. He sells the unfair advantage of looking like the category leader before you are one.

**The emotional sequence we design for:**

1. **Arrival (first 3 seconds): "Oh."** The page is calmer and more expensive-feeling than anything in their niche. No popups, no noise. They feel they have been invited somewhere slightly above their level.
2. **Recognition (first minute): "He gets our world."** Words like application, setter, pre-call page, Skool, wins channel and onboarding show up naturally. It reads as if it was written about their business without ever naming it.
3. **Play (minutes 2 to 5): "Wait, this is interactive."** They poke the funnel map, flip the before and after, try the calculator. Curiosity does the selling.
4. **The mirror (somewhere in the middle): "That's us."** The Funnel X-ray and The Translator quietly show every touchpoint most programs leave ugly. They mentally walk their own funnel and count the gaps.
5. **The math (Working together): "It's cheap, actually."** Prices are stated next to the value of one extra closed deal. One extra enrolment pays for the whole project.
6. **The exit: "It'd be stupid not to."** A small, no-risk first step (a free recorded teardown of their funnel) that makes ignoring the message feel like the wrong call.

**Tone rules for all copy:**

- First person, Sarib talking. Casual, confident, never salesy.
- Complete sentences that flow into paragraphs. No dash-broken fragments like "Apply now, it's good" split with dashes, no em dashes at all in on-page copy.
- Specific beats clever. Name real things (Typeform, Skool banner, pre-call page) instead of saying "digital assets".
- Never mock the prospect or name competitors. Point at the industry, never at them.
- Short paragraphs, generous spacing, nothing shouty, no exclamation marks.

**Words we use:** system, funnel, touchpoint, considered, calm, craft, built, shipped, the details. **Words we avoid:** synergy, cutting-edge, elevate your brand, game-changer, 10x, world-class (let the work say it), "we" (it is Sarib, not an agency voice).

## 4. Interface and information architecture

The whole thing is one app shell with six views. A fixed left sidebar, a narrow reading column (max 640px) in the centre, and panels that slide in from the right when something is opened. On mobile the sidebar collapses into a bottom sheet triggered by a small `/` button.

**Sidebar (top to bottom)**

- Wordmark: "Sarib Khan" with the live type specimen
- `/` Home
- Work
- What I build
- Working together
- About
- Notes
- Built after hours (with a small "6" count badge)
- Divider, then: "For new projects" label, email, WhatsApp
- Footer: status dot with "Booking 2 projects for November", local time in Karachi (live clock, e.g. "11:32 in Karachi"), and Follow links (Instagram, LinkedIn, Dribbble, Framer)

**Views**

| Route | View | Main interactive element |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/` | Home | Client ledger, inline client list ending in "Your program", testimonial card |
| `/work` | Work | Case files that open as a right-side panel with a clickable Funnel Map per project |
| `/build` | What I build | The Funnel X-ray (touchpoint map) and live mini demos |
| `/working-together` | Working together | Two engagement cards, the "one deal" calculator, FAQ |
| `/about` | About | Desk collage with draggable objects and captions |
| `/notes` | Notes | Short essays, including The Translator interactive |
| `/after-hours` | Built after hours | Side projects list (Wireframe's Projects page equivalent) |

**Signature interactions (the "feel the website" layer)**

1. **⌘K command menu.** Type "funnel", "price", "skool" or "book" and jump anywhere. A tiny hint in the sidebar says "Press ⌘K to look around". It signals product-level craft immediately.
2. **Funnel Map (inside each case file).** A horizontal chain of screens: Ad, Landing page, Application, Booking, Confirmation, Pre-call page, Onboarding, Community. Click a node and the screenshot swaps in with a two-line note on what was done there.
3. **Before and after slider.** Drag a handle across the old and new version of a page. Used only where Sarib has a real before.
4. **The Funnel X-ray.** A vertical map of 12 touchpoints of a coaching funnel. Each one expands to show "What most programs ship" next to "What I ship". This is the mirror moment.
5. **Live mini demos.** Small, real, working widgets: an ROI calculator, one step of an application quiz, a coach booking card. Not screenshots. They prove "custom built tools" without a paragraph.
6. **The Translator.** A list of things clients say ("Make the font bigger", "It just doesn't pop") that flip to reveal what the designer actually changed and why.
7. **Testimonial card.** One quote at a time, with the client's logo and an optional play button for a voice note or Loom clip.
8. **Desk collage.** Objects on the About page can be nudged and dragged slightly, then spring back.

**Navigation rules:** no page reloads (client-side routing with a subtle crossfade), the URL updates per view and per opened case file (e.g. `/work/ima-accelerator`) so Sarib can deep-link a specific project in a DM, and the browser back button closes panels before leaving views.

## 10. Copy: Notes (`/notes`)

Notes is where Sarib's point of view lives. It's what turns "a good designer" into "the person who understands why this works". Three short essays and one interactive. Each essay opens in the reading column with a date, a reading time and nothing else.

**Breadcrumb:** Notes

**Heading:** Things I keep explaining to clients.

### Note 1: It's all design

*3 min read*

> Every client I've worked with has said some version of the same thing. Can we make the font bigger. Can we move this up. It just doesn't pop yet. Then a few changes later they say, now it looks good.
>
> What they almost never say is why it looks good now, because they don't know, and honestly they shouldn't have to. The font wasn't too small. The hierarchy was wrong, so nothing told your eye where to go first. Moving the section up didn't fix it either. The page was answering questions in the wrong order. When it finally "pops", it's usually because the contrast, spacing and rhythm started working together.
>
> That's what design actually is. It isn't decoration on top of a business. It's every decision about what someone sees first, what they trust, and what they do next. Even an ugly page that converts has a design. Sometimes ugly is a deliberate choice, because it feels raw and honest in the right place. The difference is whether someone made that choice on purpose or it just happened.
>
> Most funnels in this space just happened. That's the opportunity.

### Note 2: A $5,000 offer in a $50 page

*2 min read*

> You can sell a $5,000 program with an ugly funnel. Lots of people do. But think about what your lead is actually doing before they book. They're deciding whether you're the real deal, and they're doing it in seconds, based on things they can't put into words.
>
> A considered funnel doesn't feel like being sold to. It feels like being taken seriously. The application feels like an invitation, the pre-call page answers the doubt before the setter has to, and the whole thing stays in their memory long after the call. That feeling is worth a lot more than the cost of designing it, and it keeps working every single day without anyone touching it.

### Note 3: The easiest advantage in coaching

*2 min read*

> Coaching programs compete on offers, on results, on testimonials and on ad spend. Those are all hard, expensive and slow to improve. Almost nobody competes on design, which means the bar is on the floor.
>
> That's good news. You don't need a new offer to look like the leader in your space. You need every touchpoint to look like it belongs to the leader. Change the design and the system around it, and you instantly look like the program everyone else is copying.

### The Translator (interactive)

**Lead-in:** What clients say, and what actually changed. Tap a card to flip it.

| What the client says | What the designer actually changed |
| --- | --- |
| "Make the font bigger." | The type scale. The headline needed more contrast against the body, not just more size. |
| "It doesn't pop." | Contrast and focus. Three things were competing for attention, so I took two away. |
| "Can we move this section up?" | The order of the story. The page answered "how much" before it answered "why should I care". |
| "It feels a bit cheap." | Spacing and alignment. Everything was almost aligned, and almost is what reads as cheap. |
| "Can we add more to this page?" | Usually the opposite. Removing two sections made the remaining ones land harder. |
| "Now it looks good." | Twenty small decisions that you'll never see individually, which is exactly the point. |

## 11. Design system and motion

Light first, like Wireframe, with a proper dark mode for people who browse at night. Restraint is the whole look: small type, lots of air, almost no colour, and one accent saved for things you can touch.

**Colour tokens**

| Token | Light | Dark | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--bg` | #FDFDFC | #0E0E0D | Page background |
| `--surface` | #F6F6F4 | #161615 | Cards, panels, sidebar |
| `--border` | #EBEBE8 | #262624 | Hairlines (1px) |
| `--text` | #111110 | #EDEDEA | Primary text |
| `--muted` | #6F6F6B | #8F8F8A | Meta, roles, captions |
| `--accent` | \[pick one: #2F5BFF calm ink blue, or #C8FF00 from the existing portfolio for dark mode only\] | same | Focus rings, active nav, status dot, demo highlights |

**Typography**

- Sans for everything: Geist or Inter, 15px body, 1.6 line height, -0.01em tracking on headings.
- Serif for moments: Instrument Serif (already in Sarib's portfolio system) for the wordmark specimen, testimonial quotes and Translator fronts.
- Mono for meta: Geist Mono at 12px for breadcrumbs, years, counters and the live clock.
- Headings stay small. H1 around 28 to 32px. Confidence comes from space and alignment, not size.

**Layout**

- Sidebar 240px fixed, reading column max 640px, right panel 520px on desktop and full screen on mobile.
- 8px spacing grid. Sections separated by 96px on desktop and 64px on mobile.
- Radius 10px on cards and 999px on small buttons. Hairline borders instead of shadows, with one soft shadow reserved for open panels.

**Motion principles**

- Everything moves like it has weight. Spring easing (stiffness around 300, damping around 30) for panels and cards, 180ms ease-out for hovers.
- View changes crossfade with a 6px upward drift. No page-level scroll animations beyond a gentle fade-in on first view.
- Micro moments that make it feel alive: the "Aa" specimen cycling every 2.5s, the status dot breathing slowly, the live Karachi clock ticking, the X-ray counter rolling up, flip cards with a real 3D flip.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: swap springs for fades and stop the cycling specimen.

**Imagery**

- Real screenshots only, captured at 2x, placed in a soft frame (surface colour, 1px border, 12px padding) so mixed screenshots still feel like one set.
- No stock photos, no 3D blobs, no AI illustrations.

**Accessibility and quality bar**

- WCAG AA contrast on all text, visible focus states, full keyboard navigation including ⌘K and the panels.
- Lighthouse 95+ on performance. Fonts self-hosted and subset, images in AVIF or WebP with blur placeholders.
- Open Graph image designed as a mini version of the home view, because the link preview is the first impression in a DM.
